Coloured Elements

Every coloured element the app has today, placed in each register’s environment — calendar layers in Day and Month, importance, blocked, overdue, done, habits, the timer, links, mentions, a block reference, callouts, labels, databases, the Road Map’s node kinds and edges. Nothing is picked by eye: each colour follows the rule beside it, from the register’s ground, text and hue.

  1. The accent is reserved. Selection, links, @mentions, the today marker and the running timer are the register's hue; every data hue keeps at least 30–40° away from it.
  2. A task is not a colour — urgency is. Five characteristics, one channel each: urgency (none / low / mid / high / urgent, and a deadline nearing raises it) is a five-step heat ladder from dim through the register's signal family to the error red — the one thing a list is scanned for; deadline is the ⚑ glyph and the date; repeating is ↻; category (self, work, a project) is the label's or database's colour on the chip and as the calendar block's fill; done is dim and struck, blocked and overdue are the error family. Two colours per task at most, each meaning one thing.
  3. Databases take any hue on the wheel — chosen by the person (a hue slider; the lightness is solved to the ground), defaulting from the title's hash over the whole circle, away from the accent — and an icon is mandatory: two databases may sit near each other in hue, their icons never do. A database date on the calendar wears its database's hue.
  4. Calendar layers: event and habit are two hues fanned ≥ 60° from the accent; a task block is its category's tint with its urgency as the stripe (no category: the surface); a database date wears its database's hue; an external (Google) event is the event hue with a dashed edge — a source is a shape, not a colour. The Month view gets dots in the same hues, which it has never had.
  5. The signal family the ladder climbs through is the register's second channel where it has one (Swiss's yellow, Playground's warm), else a solved orange-red at 22°; the ladder's steps differ in lightness and saturation, not only hue, so colour-vision deficiency reads it.
  6. The error family stays for blocked, overdue and delete: a solved red at 5° with a soft tint for chips.
  7. Labels keep their eight hues and their names; the hues are re-solved onto each ground at two levels instead of being one fixed hex in both modes.
  8. Callouts become tints of hues over the ground with the register's text on them — the fixed pastels only worked in light.
  9. Every derived colour is solved to a floor: 4.6:1 on its ground for anything read; dim 4.6, faint 3.05.
